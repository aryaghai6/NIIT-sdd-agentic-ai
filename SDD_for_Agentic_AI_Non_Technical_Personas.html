Why Spec-Driven Development for Agentic AI?

Agentic AI changes the delivery problem for non-technical roles. A chatbot mainly responds. An agent can retrieve information, make decisions, call tools, update systems, hand work to other agents and continue until a goal is reached.

A chatbotRespondsAnswers a question and stops.
→
An agentActsRetrieves, decides, calls tools, updates systems, hands off to other agents and continues until the goal is reached.

Requirements therefore cannot stop at features and user stories. Teams need a precise, business-readable specification of what the agent is expected to do, what it can access, which decisions it may take, how exceptions are handled, how quality is evaluated and when a human must intervene.

SDD in one sentence: Spec-Driven Development is the discipline of agreeing the behaviour, boundaries, evidence and acceptance conditions of an Agentic AI system before implementation, then using that specification as the reference point throughout delivery.

Eight SDD Elements

All Eight at a Glance
SDD elementPlain-English meaningWhy it matters in Agentic AI
Outcome SpecificationWhat business result should change, for whom, and by how much?Stops teams building an impressive agent with no measurable value.
Workflow SpecificationWhat steps, decisions, handoffs and exceptions make up the work?Agents need an explicit operating context, not only a task description.
Agent Role SpecificationWhich agent does what? What is each agent not allowed to do?Prevents overlapping responsibility, rogue delegation and unclear accountability.
Context & Knowledge SpecificationWhich policies, documents, data products, systems and definitions can the agent use?Reduces hallucination and inconsistent decisions.
Tool / Action SpecificationWhich systems can the agent query or update, and under what conditions?Controls operational risk when AI moves from answering to acting.
Guardrail SpecificationWhich actions are prohibited, restricted or require approval?Creates safety and compliance boundaries.
Evaluation SpecificationHow will correctness, groundedness, latency, cost and task success be measured?Makes quality measurable before launch.
Human Oversight SpecificationWhere does the human approve, review, override or own the final decision?Keeps accountability explicit.

Common real-world scenario bank

Five agentic scenarios, used across the journeys

Each scenario pairs an agentic challenge with what non-technical roles must specify. Two anchor persona capstones and one drives the cross-persona simulation.

01

Scenarios
Customer Complaint ResolutionBA capstone
Agentic challenge

Agent reads complaint, checks customer history, policy and service records, proposes resolution and may trigger a service action.

What non-technical roles must specify

Resolution rules, escalation thresholds, allowed actions, SLA, evidence and approval.

Invoice Exception ManagementPM capstone
Agentic challenge

Agent reconciles PO, invoice and receipt, identifies exception, requests missing evidence and routes approval.

What non-technical roles must specify

Exception taxonomy, financial thresholds, segregation of duties, data sources, approval path and audit record.

Employee Policy Assistant
Agentic challenge

Agent answers policy questions, identifies role/location context and escalates ambiguous cases.

What non-technical roles must specify

Source-of-truth documents, policy precedence, privacy, confidence threshold and handoff rules.

Project Risk SentinelCross-persona simulation
Agentic challenge

Agent reads plans, RAID logs, status notes and dependencies, then raises emerging delivery risks.

What non-technical roles must specify

Risk definitions, source data, false-positive tolerance, escalation and decision ownership.

Engineering Defect Triage
Agentic challenge

Agents classify defect, find similar incidents, check component history and propose routing / next action.

What non-technical roles must specify

Defect taxonomy, evidence, ownership, tool permissions, safe action limits and acceptance criteria.
